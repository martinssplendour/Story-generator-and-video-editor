One day, I’ll tell you how I lost the best man I ever met because I couldn’t stay away from the worst one. Actually, that day is today.

I met Damilola on Splendoure after joining his plan for a quiet rooftop dinner in Lagos. I nearly cancelled. I assumed he would be like every other man who looked impressive online: expensive clothes, rehearsed compliments, and nothing interesting to say.

But Dami was different. He was handsome, successful, and emotionally intelligent. He remembered the smallest things. If I casually mentioned an important meeting on Tuesday, he would call that evening to ask how it went. He never rushed me. Never pressured me. Never made me question where I stood with him. Dami was everything I claimed I wanted.

Then there was Tobi.

A few weeks later, I created another plan card on Splendoure. Some nice girls joined because they wanted a night out at a club, so we all met on Victoria Island and had a genuinely good time. That was where I met Tobi.

Tobi was loud, reckless, and completely wrong for me. He disappeared for days, replied whenever he felt like it, and only remembered my existence after midnight. But the attraction between us was ridiculous. And Tobi was gifted. Yes. Exactly what you’re thinking.

I knew he was trouble from the first night. But trouble has a strange way of feeling exciting when peace feels unfamiliar.

So I began living two lives. In one, Dami planned thoughtful dates and talked about introducing me to his family. In the other, Tobi sent messages at two in the morning: “Come outside.” And somehow, I always went.

Every time I left Tobi, I hated myself. I would sit in my car, delete our messages, and promise it was the last time. Then I would go home, put on Dami’s shirt, and answer his video call as though nothing had happened. The lies became frighteningly easy.

Until one Friday night.

Dami told me he was travelling to Abuja. Tobi messaged me fifteen minutes later. I didn’t even hesitate. But when I arrived at Tobi’s apartment, he wasn’t alone. Another woman was sitting on his sofa, wearing his shirt.

I should have walked away. Instead, I started arguing, as though I had any right to demand loyalty from the man I was using to betray someone else.

Then the lift doors opened behind me.

Dami stepped out. He hadn’t travelled anywhere. Tobi was his cousin.

The entire hallway went silent. Dami didn’t shout. He didn’t insult me. He didn’t even ask for an explanation. He just looked at me as though he no longer recognised the woman standing in front of him.

Then he said, “I kept thinking I wasn’t doing enough. I never imagined I was doing too much for someone who didn’t deserve it.”

He walked away before I could answer. That was the last time Dami spoke to me.

I called him eighty-three times. I sent paragraphs, voice notes, and apologies that became more desperate every day. He blocked me everywhere.

That was when the panic attacks started. Every time my phone rang, I thought it might be him. Every time I saw a car like his, my chest tightened. And every night, I replayed that hallway in my head, wishing I could return to the moment before those lift doors opened.

Tobi tried contacting me again. I blocked him without replying. But blocking Tobi was easy. Living with what I had done to Dami was the difficult part.

Six months later, I opened Splendoure again. Dami’s profile was gone. Maybe he found someone else, someone who valued his peace before she had to lose it.

Now, whenever I join a plan, I find myself searching for pieces of him in every man I meet. The calm voice. The thoughtful questions. The man who remembers Tuesday.

I’m back on Splendoure looking for a good man again. But deep down, I know the truth. I’m not searching for someone like Dami. I’m searching for the version of myself who wouldn’t have destroyed him.

And if I ever find another man who loves me that gently, I pray I’ve finally become the kind of woman who knows how to keep him.
